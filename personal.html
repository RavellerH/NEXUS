Personal Context Engine Research

What Does It Mean for an AI
to Truly Know You?

Every session with ChatGPT or Claude starts the same: you are a stranger. The model is brilliant, but it knows nothing about your projects, your past decisions, your thinking style, or your world. A personal context engine changes this.

Personal AI research Based on Karpathy's LLM OS Product roadmap for NEXUS Gartner 2025: "Context engineering is in"

What "Personal Context" Actually Means

Context is everything the model can see and reason over in a single call. Personal context is the subset specific to one person — their history, decisions, preferences, and world.

Dimension What it captures Example
Episodic Past events and conversations "In January I decided to use ChromaDB. Here's why."
Semantic Facts about your world, projects, people "AT-201 is the temperature transmitter on Unit 3."
Preference How you work and communicate "Farhan prefers concise answers with examples."
Temporal Time-ordered project and decision state "This project was on hold in March. Resumed April 15."
Relational Your people, their roles and context "Ahmad is the site engineer on Project Tanjung."

A system that captures and retrieves all five dimensions has personal context. Most current AI tools capture at best one or two. They're great at world knowledge; they know nothing about your world.

Personal Context Engine vs. Common LLM

The difference is not intelligence — it's knowledge of you. A common LLM is an expert stranger. A personal context engine is an expert colleague who has been working with you for years.

The Expert Stranger Problem Asking ChatGPT a question is like calling a senior consultant you've never met. They are brilliant and knowledgeable. But they don't know your specific situation, your past decisions, or your constraints. You spend the first half of every call re-explaining context. A personal context engine eliminates re-explanation.

Karpathy's LLM OS Model

Andrej Karpathy (ex-Tesla AI, ex-OpenAI, founder of Eureka Labs) framed the LLM not as a chatbot but as the CPU of a new kind of operating system. This mental model explains why context engines are necessary.

"The context window is the RAM. The weights are the slow hard disk — a vague dream of the internet. The central challenge of AI engineering is figuring out what to put in RAM at the right time."

Andrej Karpathy — paraphrased from "Intro to Large Language Models" (2023) and related talks
LLM Operating System

Hard disk→Weights (trained knowledge)slow, cannot be edited, becomes stale
Keyboard/Mouse→Text / multimodal input
Display→Generated output

Karpathy's Actual System — The LLM Wiki

Published as a GitHub Gist in early 2026. Three layers: Raw Sources (immutable, what you feed it) → Wiki Layer (LLM-maintained Markdown, entity + concept pages, cross-references) → Schema Layer (a CLAUDE.md config defining conventions).

"You rarely ever write or edit the wiki manually. It's the domain of the LLM."

Current scale: ~100 articles, ~400,000 words on one domain. Written by no one directly. Longer than most PhD dissertations. Tools: Obsidian + Obsidian Web Clipper + qmd (hybrid BM25/vector search) + Claude Code. Explicitly modeled on Vannevar Bush's 1945 Memex concept — with the LLM solving the maintenance problem Bush couldn't.

Key Karpathy Insights

InsightImplication for context engines
Weights cannot serve as a reliable knowledge store Weights hallucinate, become stale, can't be edited. Personal knowledge must come through the context window, not from weights. This is why RAG and context engines exist.
Context assembly is the core engineering challenge Deciding what to put in the context window at query time is not a retrieval problem — it's a system design problem. This is what a context engine solves.
Tools change everything An LLM with tools can affect the world, not just describe it. Personal context + tool access = the Chief of Staff AI.
Persistent personal memory makes AI qualitatively different Stateless chatbots are a transitional phase. The mature form is a system that knows you over time — Karpathy calls this the "Chief of Staff AI."
Software 2.0: learn preferences, don't hardcode them A personal context engine should learn how you work from your history — not be explicitly programmed with rules about your style or preferences.

"I want an AI that has access to everything I've ever written, thought, or experienced. That can draft emails in my voice, recall what I decided about a problem six months ago, connect a new idea to something I read two years ago."

Andrej Karpathy — described as the "Chief of Staff AI" in interviews, 2023–2025

Personal Knowledge Base — The Foundation

A personal context engine needs something to draw on. The personal knowledge base is that foundation — your curated, linked store of thinking, decisions, and knowledge.

TL;DR A PKB is not Notion + AI. It is a structured graph of atomic ideas, linked by meaning, built up over time. Without a well-structured PKB, a personal context engine has nothing meaningful to ground its answers in.

Zettelkasten (Luhmann)

One claim per note. Written in your own words. Linked to at least one other note. No hierarchy — only links. Structure emerges from connections, not folders. Luhmann produced 70+ books using this system over 40 years.

PARA (Tiago Forte)

Projects–Areas–Resources–Archives. Action-oriented organization. Everything captured in the context of what you're currently working on. Complementary to Zettelkasten — PARA for actions, Zettelkasten for permanent insight.

Maps of Content (Nick Milo)

A hub note that lists and links other notes on a theme, without containing their content. MOCs surface knowledge clusters without imposing hierarchy. They are the navigation layer of the Zettelkasten.

Progressive Summarization

Layer 0: raw capture. Layer 1: bold key passages. Layer 2: highlight the best. Layer 3: executive summary. Layer 4: permanent note. Only go deeper on notes you return to. Defeats "saved but never read."

The 10K Note Threshold

Most practitioners stop at 10,000 notes. At this scale, SQLite FTS5 + a small embedding model retrieves in under 30ms on a laptop. No Qdrant, no Docker, no distributed system needed. The constraint is discipline, not technology.

Atomic Claim Indexing

The right unit to index is not a document or paragraph — it is a single verifiable claim. "Hybrid RRF outperforms dense-only by 580% on Recall@10" is a retrievable, linkable unit. "Retrieval" is not. Atomic claim = the Zettelkasten note = the RAG chunk that actually works.

How a Personal Context Engine Works

The architecture has five layers. The distinguishing one — what separates it from a document Q&A system — is the personal ontology and the context assembly layer.

1. Ingestion

Notes · Decisions · Conversations · Voice memos → transcribed → stored with timestamp, source, project tag. The capture must be frictionless — if it's work, it won't happen.

2. Indexing — Atomic Claims + Personal Ontology

Each ingested item → extract atomic claims → extract entities (people, projects, instruments) → build relationships (Ahmad WORKS_ON Tanjung, AT-201 LOCATED_IN Unit 3) → temporal markers (when this was known). The personal ontology is the model of your world.

3. Memory Stores

Working memory (current conversation) · Episodic (past conversations, Zep+Graphiti) · Semantic (personal ontology, vector+graph) · Procedural (how you work, learned from patterns).

4. Context Assembly — The Core Differentiator

For a query: expand query with personal ontology → retrieve from all stores → assemble context window: [identity block ~200 tokens] + [retrieved knowledge ~2,000 tokens] + [episodic context ~500 tokens] + [temporal state ~200 tokens] + [query ~100 tokens]. The assembly budget forces intelligent ranking.

5. Generation — Grounded and Cited

LLM generates response grounded in assembled personal context. Every answer cites the specific note/decision/document it drew from. Hallucination is flagged when the system lacks relevant personal knowledge.

The 10× Moments

When does a personal context engine feel obviously superior to a common LLM? These are the moments when the difference becomes visceral, not theoretical.

01

Why did we decide this?

You: "Why are we using ChromaDB instead of Qdrant?"
Common LLM:
Generic comparison of ChromaDB vs Qdrant features...
Personal context engine:
"You chose ChromaDB in Nov 2025 — simplest option for Phase 1. You noted you'd likely migrate to Qdrant when you need filtered metadata search."
02

What did I miss while away?

You: "I haven't touched the reranking module in 2 months. Catch me up."
Common LLM:
Here's a general overview of reranking methods...
Personal context engine:
"Since March: you added D14 (BGE-v2-M3 for multilingual), flagged an audit issue with AT-201 under semantic reranking, and have Q-open on exact match layer integration."
03

Write this in my voice

You: "Draft a WhatsApp message to Ahmad about the AT-201 delay."
Common LLM:
"Dear Ahmad, I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to reach out regarding..."
Personal context engine:
"Ahmad, AT-201 calibration delayed 3 days — waiting on vendor response. Will update once confirmed. Should still hit April 20 milestone."
04

Connect things across months

You: "Why does the FTS5 hybrid index matter for us specifically?"
Common LLM:
FTS5 provides full-text search with BM25 ranking...
Personal context engine:
"The GA02 audit finding (Dec 2025) showed your dense embeddings couldn't distinguish AT-201 from AT-202. FTS5 was the fix — it's in your D-hybrid decision."
05

Where to focus next

You: "I'm starting Phase 1 implementation today. Where do I begin?"
Common LLM:
To start a software implementation, first define your requirements...
Personal context engine:
"3 critical bugs (C02: missing module specs) block Phase 1. 2 open questions (Q4, Q5) unresolved. Audit says: fix C01–C05 before writing code."

NEXUS: The Road to Personal Context Engine

NEXUS currently builds a project-scoped context engine for EPC teams. The personal context engine is the destination — a Chief of Staff AI for a PM. Here's the progression:

Phase 1–2 · Current

Project Context Engine

Knows: documents, specs, SOPs for one project. Can answer: "what does the AT-201 data sheet say?"

Phase 3

Team Context Engine

Knows: cross-project patterns, team decisions, role-specific context. Can answer: "how did we handle a similar issue last project?"

Phase 4

Personal Layer

Knows: PM's decision history, preferences, communication style. Can answer: "given how you work, here's what I recommend."

Future

Chief of Staff AI

Knows: everything + active work state. Proactively: "you have 3 unresolved questions from last week that are now blocking Phase 2."

Karpathy's framing predicts this roadmap The LLM OS model says the context window is RAM — and the filesystem (personal KB) is what makes it intelligent. NEXUS Phase 1 builds the filesystem for a project. The personal context engine phase builds the filesystem for a person. The LLM stays the same; what changes is the richness and continuity of what's in RAM.
