The problem

The knowledge exists. It just can't be found.

A typical EPC project generates thousands of messages, dozens of documents, and hundreds of decisions. They live in different places with no common thread. Every question becomes a search expedition.

"What's the operating pressure spec for AT-201?"
Check the P&ID. Check the datasheet. Check the WhatsApp group where the vendor replied. Check the ECO from two months ago that superseded the original spec. Give up and ask the site engineer directly.
"What did we decide about the pump vendor last March?"
Scroll through 3,000 messages in the procurement group chat. The person who made the call left the project. Their reasoning went with them.
"Does the SOP match what the site team actually does?"
The SOP is v2. The site team is working off a WhatsApp message that updated it. Nobody knows if the update was ever formalized. Both versions exist simultaneously.
The solution

One place that knows your project.

NEXUS ingests your project's documents and chats, indexes them with context, and answers questions with citations — pointing you to the exact source: which document, which page, which message, which author.

Why this exists

A note from the builder.

EPC projects in Southeast Asia run on institutional knowledge that lives entirely in informal channels. The official documents are always 2 weeks behind the real decisions, which happened in a WhatsApp group at 11pm. When a field engineer asks a question that was answered 3 months ago, nobody can find it, so they ask again — and the PM answers from memory, which may or may not match the actual spec.

NEXUS is my attempt to solve that problem without asking a client to trust their sensitive project data to a cloud service. It runs on their own hardware, in their own network, and it never phones home. No subscription dashboard watching their usage. No risk of a provider going dark and taking their data with them.

I'm building this in public. The full architecture, all design decisions, the audit findings — everything is documented in the links below. If you're an EPC PM in SEA and this sounds like your problem, reach out. I'm looking for early feedback from real teams.
